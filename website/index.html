Zen Mode

It doesn't ask for a desktop.

Most chat apps want a whole monitor to themselves. Overzen shrinks down to a small, borderless pane that stays on top of every other window — your editor, your terminal, your browser — and drags anywhere you put it.

Slide the opacity down until it's barely there, or bring it back to full strength when you need to read a long reply. One click returns it to a normal window.

Built to stay out of the way

Three quiet decisions

Any model, one window

Point it at Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, or Jan running on your machine — or hand it an OpenAI-compatible base URL, model name, and key for a cloud provider instead. Switch sources from the same settings panel, anytime.

Nothing leaves without asking

Local models never touch the network. If you do add a cloud key, it's encrypted at rest with your operating system's own keychain — Overzen only ever remembers whether a key is saved, never the key itself.

A PIN, not a password

Turn on App Lock and a four-digit PIN keeps the window closed to anyone else at your desk. The PIN is hashed and salted before it's stored — Overzen couldn't show it back to you even if it wanted to.
